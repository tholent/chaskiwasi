# What the device knows about you

This is a plain description of what your device does and doesn't collect
about you, and where it goes. It's written so you can read it yourself, not
just have it explained to you.

## Your guardians can read everything you send and receive

Your letters live in an email mailbox your guardians control. That's true
the whole time you use this device — not just for letters they've decided
to look at, but for all of them, the same way it would be if you were
using a normal email account they had the password to. This isn't hidden
or a setting that can be changed. If that's news to you, it's better to
know it plainly now than to find out later.

## There's no tracking that follows you around

The device does **not** constantly report where you are. There's no
background location tracking running on it, ever, and nothing you can turn
on or off changes that — it was simply never built to do it.

The only way your location ever leaves the device is if **you** choose to
attach it to a letter you're writing — like adding a return address to a
postcard. If you don't do that, your location is never sent anywhere by
this device.

**One honest limit, regardless of anything above:** this device having no
location feature doesn't make you untraceable. Your cell carrier already
knows which tower your phone-shaped device is talking to, the same way it
would for any phone — that's just how cellular networks work, and nothing
about this device changes it. This document can only tell you what *this
device* does; it can't promise more than that.

## Basic status is collected, and gets deleted on its own

The device reports basic status — things like battery level, signal
strength, and how full its outbox is — each time it connects. Your
guardians can see the most recent report of this in their web site, mostly
so they can tell if the device is alive and reachable.

This status information is kept for a limited time (a matter of weeks) and
then automatically and permanently deleted. None of it ever gets copied
into your letter archive, and none of it is kept when you eventually get
full, unrestricted control of your own account (see below). Nobody is
handed a log of how your battery was doing years from now.

## Your letters are yours to keep

Every letter you write or receive stays in the mailbox for good — nothing
is ever deleted or rewritten by the device. When you're old enough that the
restrictions on this device no longer make sense, the mailbox becomes a
normal email account, fully yours, with your whole letter history intact
from the very first one. The status information described above does not
come with it — that part is designed to be forgotten. Your letters are
not.
